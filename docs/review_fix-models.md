## H1: Keyes' and Cortana's heads cut open in a10's opening (restoration, on)

**Cause [D], [U] until neardump reads back.** nearauto moves the near plane out to x2..x16 when nothing is near the eye. A model's bounding sphere that contains the eye normally holds the plane at x1, except while the player is seated (the vehicle he drives contains the eye and must not hold the plane in). In a10's opening the player rides the cryo tube, so `psp_player_seated()` said 1, and the spheres around the cinematic camera (Keyes', Cortana's) were dropped as if they were his seat. The hand test's log shows the plane at x16 through the cinematic with no model capping it.

**Fix** (`psp_fixups.py`, players.c's `psp_player_seated`): in a cinematic the camera belongs to the script, not the seat, so the function answers 0 there, as on foot:

```c
if (!seated_in_cinematics && cinematic_in_progress())
	return 0;
```

Keep word `nearseatcine`: the old answer. Cost: one call per frame; the plane may now sit at x1 during close-ups (coarser depth there, as on foot beside a model). At a10 107-117 s (the cryo room, light-bar test) both sides read x1, `seated 0`: the cinematic is over by then [E]. Across the opening cinematic (game time 2-95 s: the flyover, the bridge, Keyes' first close-ups), before against fixed: `seated 0` on every shot frame on both sides, neardump identical, Keyes' head whole in every close-up on both sides; the plane sits at x1-x4 in the close-ups, each time with a model near enough to cap it (e.g. x4 with the nearest sphere 0.78 away) [E]. So in this window the fix changes nothing, and nothing needed it. The hand test's near-plane counts over the same scene match the emulator's (x16 only over the flyover, x1-x4 with models capping on the bridge) [M]. Game time 96-192 s (the rest of the bridge scene and the cryo room, a shot every 4 s, neardump): `seated 0` and the plane at x1 in every close-up on both sides, Keyes whole; before and fixed differ only in see-through pixels (H3's one-sided change), never in the near plane [E]. Cortana fell between those shots. PENDING: her close-up at one shot a second (game time 100-121 s). Verdict so far: the emulator never puts the player in a seat through a10's cinematics, so the hand test's x16 (seated 1) isn't reproduced; the fix removes the path that gave it [D].

## H3: Cortana inside out (restoration, on) and her clipping tint (bake)

**EBOOT [D].** The Xbox culls see-through geometry's back faces unless the shader is two-sided (`rasterizer_xbox_transparent_geometry.c`: `D3DCULL_CCW` unless the two-sided flag). The port drew every see-through part from both sides, so Cortana's back faces were added over her front: inside out, and brighter. Now a one-sided flag rides bit 15 of the queue's blend word (`psp_seethrough.h`, `PSP_SEETHROUGH_ONE_SIDED_BIT`), set in `psp_transparent_map` for generic and chicago shaders (flags byte +41 bit 2) and in FIXES_MODELS `#glasscull` for glass (+40 bit 2); `psp_ge.c` turns culling on for those draws in `transparent_flush_body`. Keep word `seethroughtwosided`: both sides, as before.

**Bake [D].** cortanafade (the tint that matches the Xbox's clipping of her noise mask x4) returns, with the fault that failed four maps' bakes fixed: `generic_result` was handed other maps' texel samples of a different size from the drawn map's (2048 against 4096 rows). `_shuffled(texels, i, n)` now gives each other map exactly n rows (its own seeded order, repeated or cut). The a10 + ui bake now completes [E] and logs Cortana's tints (`cortana_body` ffc95cd9 doubled, `cortana_hair` 69d100d1 doubled, `cortana vol` 008b8768 doubled). PENDING: her close-ups, old against new, on that bake.

**Read back [E]** (a10, game time 96-192 s, a shot every 4 s): before (`seethroughtwosided`) against fixed, 11 of 24 shots differ, all in see-through parts: the bridge's hologram grid around Keyes (24,362 pixels: its back faces gone, as the Xbox culls them) and the bridge's green glass panels (16-1,954 pixels). The fm-a10 bake against b139a's: pixel-identical in all 24 shots (Cortana isn't in any of them, and nothing else in view carries a changed tint). PENDING: her close-up at one shot a second.

## H34: no flashlight under active camouflage (the original's behaviour; no change)

[D] `object_lights.c` fades every light of a camouflaged unit by (1 - camouflage) unless the light tag sets "don't fade active camouflage". The port doesn't touch this code: the flashlight going dark is Halo's own behaviour. ChupathingyCE's PC build runs the same `object_lights.c` unchanged, so the PC reference behaves the same [D]. The emulator check (a10, flashlight then `cheat_active_camouflage`) was inconclusive: at those ticks the player stood in a lit room where the beam isn't visible; it changes nothing either way.

## H35: camouflaged Elites invisible but for their weapon (Sheepdog01's call, off)

The Xbox draws active camouflage as a refraction of what's behind; the GE can't sample the frame it draws into cheaply, so the port draws the model faint (15% alpha, no depth write), which reads as invisible. `camoshimmer` (opt-in): the camouflaged parts drawn as their own map added faintly (`CAMO_SHIMMER_LEVEL` 48 of 255), unlit, drifting per frame. The a10 A/B drew no shimmering parts (`camouflaged parts shimmering 0` on both sides) [E]: the player's own camouflage shows only first-person arms, which don't take this path. PENDING: an A/B with a camouflaged Elite in view.

## H36: powerups lack their shader decorations (restoration, on)

[D] generic and chicago shaders' extra layers (the +72 block) were never drawn; the Xbox draws them after their shader (`transparent_geometry_layer_draw`). FIXES_MODELS `#layers` queues them (`psp_transparent_layers`, depth 2). Keep word `nolayers`. It is right, but it isn't H36: on b30's camouflage powerups `extra layers queued 0` [E], because neither of their shaders has layers [D]: `powerup glass` is a glass shader (type 8, no layers block), `active camoflage` a generic one with four maps (`shield noise` twice, `shield offset`, `shield mask`) and no layers. The port draws one map of a generic shader (`shield mask`, tinted by the bake), so the animated noise turning over the sphere, the decoration, is the part left out. **fix-alpha's composites don't cover it [E]**: b30's camouflage powerup up close, fix-alpha's build on its alpha3 bake with composites on against `nocomposite`: 0, 1, 1 and 21 pixels apart over four shots (the HUD), the powerups the same on both sides and the same as fix-models on b139a. The shader isn't composited (its noise scrolls under a mask that doesn't, which a still texture can't hold), so H36 isn't fixed by fix-alpha.

**genericnoise (opt-in, Sheepdog01's call).** The GE has one texture stage and a 565 frame buffer (no destination alpha), so the combiners' product (noise times mask) can't be drawn exactly. `psp_transparent_noise` (FIXES_MODELS `#genericnoise`) queues a second draw of the same triangles with the shader's first other map (not a cube map, not the drawn map's bitmap), its own scale, offset and animation (`psp_transparent_map` with `psp_transparent_map_forced`), blended as the shader blends, at `GENERIC_NOISE_LEVEL` (0.5) of its fade, untinted. Generic shaders only, not numeric counters, not meters; objects only. It's an added pass, not the product, so it is a look call: off by default, keep word `genericnoise`. The GE's models line counts the passes. PENDING: the A/B at the powerup and on Cortana (whose shaders are generic with noise maps too), and its cost.

## H39: b30's Pelican off its path while its markers keep the place

[D] poseahead is exact by design (all RAM written back before the push; animation pages written back after reads). The repro needs b30's extraction script: `scriptsdump` (recon builds) names it, then the console reaches it with `poseaheadoff` / `poseaheadcheck`. b30's scripts include `extraction_build` (static), `pelican_fade` (dormant), `cutscene_extraction_exit` (static) [E]. On b30's opening flight `poseaheadcheck` compared 72,782 poses made ahead with the main CPU's: 0 differed [E]. The extraction Pelican, `extraction_build` called at the hand test's spot (the hand test's camera within 0.7 m), 90 s of game time with it hovering overhead [E]:
- poseahead default against `poseaheadoff`: the shots differ only in the HUD (11-42 pixels a shot); the Pelican is whole, its markings on the hull, on both;
- `poseaheadcheck`: 199,833 poses made ahead compared with the main CPU's, 0 differed;
- `cutscene_extraction_exit` (the cinematic): the Pelican whole through the climb.

Verdict: **not reproduced from a fresh load; poseahead cleared.** The hand test reached the Pelican after about 25 minutes of play, with the object render-state cache full (512 of 512, the oldest pushed out each view) and geometry streaming [M]; the emulator's fresh load has neither. The hand test's shots show the hull pitched while its lettering and strips (separate parts of the same model) keep the scripted place: parts of one model drawn from different node matrices, which points at per-part state that survives a cache's turnover rather than at the pose itself [U]. With the render-state cache halved (`renderstates256`, `poseaheadcheck`), the same call: the cache filled (256 of 256) on the way, but over the Pelican's stretch only 0.0-0.8 render states a view were worked out whole, pushing out the oldest; 145,097-199,833 poses checked, 0 differed; the Pelican level and whole, its markings on the hull, in all 16 shots [E]. The hand test's own log at the moment (game time 3,337-3,356 s) shows the object list's peak 104-107 of 256 and 0.0 render states pushed out a view [M]: the cache wasn't turning over when the hull split, so the cache is cleared too. Verdict: **not reproduced in the emulator; neither poseahead's results nor the render-state cache explain it.** What the emulator can't show: the Media Engine's cache on hardware (poses made ahead are written by the ME; the emulator's ME shares the main CPU's memory view), and streaming at the moment (the hand log reads 1-2 animation pages and 25-121 geometry blocks in each 5 s around it) [U]. Next repro: on a PSP, `extraction_build` at the hand test's spot, default against `poseaheadoff` (one job, `keepsets`), Sheepdog01's call on hardware time. No change on this branch.
