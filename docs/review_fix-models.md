## H1: Keyes' and Cortana's heads cut open in a10's opening (restoration, on)

**Cause [D], [U] until neardump reads back.** nearauto moves the near plane out to x2..x16 when nothing is near the eye. A model's bounding sphere that contains the eye normally holds the plane at x1, except while the player is seated (the vehicle he drives contains the eye and must not hold the plane in). In a10's opening the player rides the cryo tube, so `psp_player_seated()` said 1, and the spheres around the cinematic camera (Keyes', Cortana's) were dropped as if they were his seat. The hand test's log shows the plane at x16 through the cinematic with no model capping it.

**Fix** (`psp_fixups.py`, players.c's `psp_player_seated`): in a cinematic the camera belongs to the script, not the seat, so the function answers 0 there, as on foot:

```c
if (!seated_in_cinematics && cinematic_in_progress())
	return 0;
```

Keep word `nearseatcine`: the old answer. Cost: one call per frame; the plane may now sit at x1 during close-ups (coarser depth there, as on foot beside a model). At a10 107-117 s (the cryo room, light-bar test) both sides read x1, `seated 0`: the cinematic is over by then [E]. Across the opening cinematic (game time 2-95 s: the flyover, the bridge, Keyes' first close-ups), before against fixed: `seated 0` on every shot frame on both sides, neardump identical, Keyes' head whole in every close-up on both sides; the plane sits at x1-x4 in the close-ups, each time with a model near enough to cap it (e.g. x4 with the nearest sphere 0.78 away) [E]. So in this window the fix changes nothing, and nothing needed it. The hand test's near-plane counts over the same scene match the emulator's (x16 only over the flyover, x1-x4 with models capping on the bridge) [M]. Game time 96-192 s (the rest of the bridge scene and the cryo room, a shot every 4 s, neardump): `seated 0` and the plane at x1 in every close-up on both sides, Keyes whole; before and fixed differ only in see-through pixels (H3's one-sided change), never in the near plane [E]. Cortana fell between those shots. Her close-up at one shot a second (game time 100-121 s): before (`seethroughtwosided`) against fixed, 6 of 21 shots differ (228-7,790 pixels), all on her hologram: before, her back faces (hair, the far side of the face) add over the front, brighter and inside out; fixed, she is lighter and whole, front faces only, as the Xbox culls [E]. Near plane x1, `seated 0` throughout. A hard vertical seam stays across her head and shoulder in the frames where she materialises (game ~54 s on the shots' clock), on both sides: the right part of the head and body shows a dark rectangle with the wall behind it. It isn't the see-through change and isn't the near plane; its cause is open (a clip or scissor on the hologram's projection?) [U]. Verdict so far: the emulator never puts the player in a seat through a10's cinematics, so the hand test's x16 (seated 1) isn't reproduced; the fix removes the path that gave it [D].

## H3: Cortana inside out (restoration, on) and her clipping tint (bake)

**EBOOT [D].** The Xbox culls see-through geometry's back faces unless the shader is two-sided (`rasterizer_xbox_transparent_geometry.c`: `D3DCULL_CCW` unless the two-sided flag). The port drew every see-through part from both sides, so Cortana's back faces were added over her front: inside out, and brighter. Now a one-sided flag rides bit 15 of the queue's blend word (`psp_seethrough.h`, `PSP_SEETHROUGH_ONE_SIDED_BIT`), set in `psp_transparent_map` for generic and chicago shaders (flags byte +41 bit 2) and in FIXES_MODELS `#glasscull` for glass (+40 bit 2); `psp_ge.c` turns culling on for those draws in `transparent_flush_body`. Keep word `seethroughtwosided`: both sides, as before.

**Bake [D].** cortanafade (the tint that matches the Xbox's clipping of her noise mask x4) returns, with the fault that failed four maps' bakes fixed: `generic_result` was handed other maps' texel samples of a different size from the drawn map's (2048 against 4096 rows). `_shuffled(texels, i, n)` now gives each other map exactly n rows (its own seeded order, repeated or cut). The a10 + ui bake now completes [E] and logs Cortana's tints (`cortana_body` ffc95cd9 doubled, `cortana_hair` 69d100d1 doubled, `cortana vol` 008b8768 doubled). **Read back [E]** (a10, game time 100-121 s, a shot a second): the fm-a10 bake against b139a's is still pixel-identical with Cortana in 10 of the 21 shots, and both log 34 see-through tints, so the bake's tint change doesn't reach her on screen: her hologram is the same magenta on both. Open: whether b139a's bake already carried that tint, or whether it isn't applied to her shaders at all (not settled).

**Read back [E]** (a10, game time 96-192 s, a shot every 4 s): before (`seethroughtwosided`) against fixed, 11 of 24 shots differ, all in see-through parts: the bridge's hologram grid around Keyes (24,362 pixels: its back faces gone, as the Xbox culls them) and the bridge's green glass panels (16-1,954 pixels). The fm-a10 bake against b139a's: pixel-identical in all 24 shots (Cortana isn't in any of them, and nothing else in view carries a changed tint). Her close-up at one shot a second is read below (H3's Cortana section).

## H3 follow-up: the seam across Cortana while she materialises (restoration, on)

**What [E].** At a10's bridge close-up a hard vertical edge cut her head and shoulder (the step is in the same screen columns on both sides of the one-sided change: not the culling, not the near plane).

**Why [D].** Her biped's tag sets "transparency self occludes", so `render_objects.c` draws her with the modifier effect (type 2), and the Xbox draws such an object in two goes (`rasterizer_xbox_transparent_geometry.c`, `_render_model_effect_type_cortana`): every see-through part of hers writes depth only (colour writes off, no culling, `LESSEQUAL`), then the parts draw as usual, so only her nearest surface shows. `models.c`'s cortana hack (a scenario flag, her model's node checksum) also sorts all her parts after every other see-through group (`rasterizer_transparent_geometry.c`, `cortana_tiebreak`). The port did neither: her hair's and head's back layers blended over her face in the order her parts sorted, and where two parts meet the order changes: the seam [U until the A/B].

**Change.** `psp_fixups.py` passes the cortana hack in the effect type's second byte; `psp_ge_model_effect` numbers each self-occluding model (`model_occluder`) and its see-through parts carry the number (`transparent_draw.occluder`; a generic shader with ignore-effect, flags +41 bit 5, is left out as `shader_ignores_effect` leaves it out). `transparent_later` sorts the hack's parts last. In `transparent_flush_body`, the first draw of a run of one object's parts calls `transparent_occluder_depth`: the run's geometry with `sceGuPixelMask(0xFFFFFFFF)`, texturing off, depth writes on, both sides; then the parts draw as before and pass `GEQUAL` only at the nearest depth (the same vertices and matrix, so the same depth). First-person parts are left out of the depth go. Keep word `noselfocclude`: as before. The log's models line counts "self-occluding objects' depth first N times, M parts".

**Cost.** Her geometry drawn once more, depth only, while she is on screen; no CPU placing (the placed vertices are reused). PENDING (fixmodels6): a10 game 100-121 s, profile and heartbeat frame ms off against on.

**A/B.** PENDING (fixmodels6): `a10-occl-off` (noselfocclude) against `a10-occl-on`, a shot a second; `a10-occl-tdump` lists her draws if the seam stays.

## H3: her tint (bake): settled [D]

b139a's bake was made from a commit without cortanafade (it was reverted for b139), and the fm-a10 bake carries it: `cortana_body` ffcd5cdf on b139a, ffc95cd9 on fm-a10 (the hair's and the volume's tints are the same on both; their shaders don't scale an output up). So the tint is applied; it moves her body's red and blue by 4 and 6 of 255, and her body's map is dark (mean 0.04 0.00 0.12): drawn doubled into the 565 framebuffer that change is far below one step, which is why the shots are pixel-identical. Nothing to fix in the bake. PENDING (fixmodels6, `a10-occl-notint`): that her draws take the pack's tint at all (notint against on should differ on her).

## H34: no flashlight under active camouflage (the original's behaviour; no change)

[D] `object_lights.c` fades every light of a camouflaged unit by (1 - camouflage) unless the light tag sets "don't fade active camouflage". The port doesn't touch this code: the flashlight going dark is Halo's own behaviour. ChupathingyCE's PC build runs the same `object_lights.c` unchanged, so the PC reference behaves the same [D]. The emulator check (a10, flashlight then `cheat_active_camouflage`) was inconclusive: at those ticks the player stood in a lit room where the beam isn't visible; it changes nothing either way.

## H35: camouflaged Elites invisible but for their weapon (Sheepdog01's call, off)

The Xbox draws active camouflage as a refraction of what's behind; the GE can't sample the frame it draws into cheaply, so the port draws the model faint (15% alpha, no depth write), which reads as invisible. `camoshimmer` (opt-in): the camouflaged parts drawn as their own map added faintly (`CAMO_SHIMMER_LEVEL` 48 of 255), unlit, drifting per frame. The a10 A/B drew no shimmering parts (`camouflaged parts shimmering 0` on both sides) [E]: the player's own camouflage shows only first-person arms, which don't take this path. The b30 A/B (shaft A's return spot, `mission_shafta_inv` woken at tick 2200, shots every 3 s, game 75-135 s) drew no camouflaged Elite either: `camouflaged parts shimmering 0` and all 6 shots pixel-identical, camoshimmer on against off [E]. The woken script didn't put a stealth Elite in view (the player was on the beach at the end). PENDING: an A/B at the right encounter (other dormant scripts: `mission_shafta`, `cont_shafta_entrance_inv`).

## H36: powerups lack their shader decorations (restoration, on)

[D] generic and chicago shaders' extra layers (the +72 block) were never drawn; the Xbox draws them after their shader (`transparent_geometry_layer_draw`). FIXES_MODELS `#layers` queues them (`psp_transparent_layers`, depth 2). Keep word `nolayers`. It is right, but it isn't H36: on b30's camouflage powerups `extra layers queued 0` [E], because neither of their shaders has layers [D]: `powerup glass` is a glass shader (type 8, no layers block), `active camoflage` a generic one with four maps (`shield noise` twice, `shield offset`, `shield mask`) and no layers. The port draws one map of a generic shader (`shield mask`, tinted by the bake), so the animated noise turning over the sphere, the decoration, is the part left out. **fix-alpha's composites don't cover it [E]**: b30's camouflage powerup up close, fix-alpha's build on its alpha3 bake with composites on against `nocomposite`: 0, 1, 1 and 21 pixels apart over four shots (the HUD), the powerups the same on both sides and the same as fix-models on b139a. The shader isn't composited (its noise scrolls under a mask that doesn't, which a still texture can't hold), so H36 isn't fixed by fix-alpha.

**genericnoise (opt-in, Sheepdog01's call).** The GE has one texture stage and a 565 frame buffer (no destination alpha), so the combiners' product (noise times mask) can't be drawn exactly. `psp_transparent_noise` (FIXES_MODELS `#genericnoise`) queues a second draw of the same triangles with the shader's first other map (not a cube map, not the drawn map's bitmap), its own scale, offset and animation (`psp_transparent_map` with `psp_transparent_map_forced`), blended as the shader blends, at `GENERIC_NOISE_LEVEL` (0.5) of its fade, untinted. Generic shaders only, not numeric counters, not meters; objects only. It's an added pass, not the product, so it is a look call: off by default, keep word `genericnoise`. The GE's models line counts the passes. **A/B [E]:** at b30's camouflage powerup the pass never fired (`second map passes 0` on and off, 0 of 4 shots differ, cost nil): the powerup's `active camoflage` shader isn't reaching `psp_transparent_noise`, so H36 is not visible with it yet and the cause is open. On Cortana's stretch (a10, game 100-121 s) it ran (791-2,100 passes in 300 frames) but changed only 6-50 pixels a shot: nothing visible. Cost on a10: `rasterizer_model_transparent_geometry_submit` +0.16-0.23 ms a frame (1.01/1.02/1.74 to 1.24/1.18/1.93 over three windows), `rasterizer_model_draw` unchanged; emulator wall-clock figures, indicative, not the cache model.


**Why genericnoise never fired on the powerup [D].** `psp_transparent_noise` asked the pack which map is drawn (`psp_tex_shader_map`); the pack lists only shaders whose drawn map the bake chose over the runtime's smallest-scale rule, and the powerup's `active camoflage` (map 3 drawn, the rule's own choice) isn't among them, so the call answered -1 and the pass returned before queueing anything. Cortana's shaders are listed, which is why it fired on her. The pass now applies the rule itself when the pack has no entry. Still opt-in (`genericnoise`, a look call). PENDING (fixmodels6): `b30-camo-gn-off` against `-on` at the powerup, its passes and frame ms.

## H39: b30's Pelican off its path while its markers keep the place

[D] poseahead is exact by design (all RAM written back before the push; animation pages written back after reads). The repro needs b30's extraction script: `scriptsdump` (recon builds) names it, then the console reaches it with `poseaheadoff` / `poseaheadcheck`. b30's scripts include `extraction_build` (static), `pelican_fade` (dormant), `cutscene_extraction_exit` (static) [E]. On b30's opening flight `poseaheadcheck` compared 72,782 poses made ahead with the main CPU's: 0 differed [E]. The extraction Pelican, `extraction_build` called at the hand test's spot (the hand test's camera within 0.7 m), 90 s of game time with it hovering overhead [E]:
- poseahead default against `poseaheadoff`: the shots differ only in the HUD (11-42 pixels a shot); the Pelican is whole, its markings on the hull, on both;
- `poseaheadcheck`: 199,833 poses made ahead compared with the main CPU's, 0 differed;
- `cutscene_extraction_exit` (the cinematic): the Pelican whole through the climb.

Verdict: **not reproduced from a fresh load; poseahead cleared.** The hand test reached the Pelican after about 25 minutes of play, with the object render-state cache full (512 of 512, the oldest pushed out each view) and geometry streaming [M]; the emulator's fresh load has neither. The hand test's shots show the hull pitched while its lettering and strips (separate parts of the same model) keep the scripted place: parts of one model drawn from different node matrices, which points at per-part state that survives a cache's turnover rather than at the pose itself [U]. With the render-state cache halved (`renderstates256`, `poseaheadcheck`), the same call: the cache filled (256 of 256) on the way, but over the Pelican's stretch only 0.0-0.8 render states a view were worked out whole, pushing out the oldest; 145,097-199,833 poses checked, 0 differed; the Pelican level and whole, its markings on the hull, in all 16 shots [E]. The hand test's own log at the moment (game time 3,337-3,356 s) shows the object list's peak 104-107 of 256 and 0.0 render states pushed out a view [M]: the cache wasn't turning over when the hull split, so the cache is cleared too. Verdict: **not reproduced in the emulator; neither poseahead's results nor the render-state cache explain it.** What the emulator can't show: the Media Engine's cache on hardware (poses made ahead are written by the ME; the emulator's ME shares the main CPU's memory view), and streaming at the moment (the hand log reads 1-2 animation pages and 25-121 geometry blocks in each 5 s around it) [U]. Next repro: on a PSP, `extraction_build` at the hand test's spot, default against `poseaheadoff` (one job, `keepsets`), Sheepdog01's call on hardware time. No change on this branch.
