# Measured hardware facts

Every hardware figure the port has measured, in one place, each with the line it came from. `lab:` citations point into the lab repository, most often its findings table `psp/sierra/findings/measurements.tsv`, quoted with the row's fields joined by ` | `. The design docs hold the reasoning; this page holds the numbers. Read it before sizing a lever, writing a part's budget or briefing a helper.

**How to read it**
- Labels follow the repo's rule: [M] measured on the PSP, [E] emulator or estimate, [D] read in code, data or a vendor manual, [U] inference. "[M on E]" is a PSP measurement scaled by an emulator count.
- A citation is `file:line` in `docs/` (or `lab:` for the lab repository) and a fragment of that line, so it can be found again after the line moves. Lines are as of `docs/cost-map` at 5f1eb7d (2026-11-09).
- Where two measurements disagree, both stay, marked **CONFLICT**, with the conditions that differ.
- PSP-1000 unless a bullet names the PSP-3000. The two agree on almost everything measured on both (last section).

**How it was made (2026-11-09):** 942 candidate lines marked [M] or about hardware were pulled mechanically from the docs; a read-only DeepSeek pass grouped them; every citation was then checked against its file (all 188 found; a few differ from the page only by a dash or emphasis). Port facts (buffer sizes, per-map timings, levers) were left out. A second pass the same day compared the 450 hardware rows of the lab's findings tables against the page: it settled ten open items and added ~45 facts that had lived only in those tables. A new hardware measurement adds or updates its bullet here in the same commit as its write-up.

## Main CPU

- **Allegrex: MIPS32 R2-like, 7-stage pipeline, 32-bit, no MMU/TLB; 222 MHz firmware default, 333 MHz allowed (the port runs 333).** [M] `psp_hardware_reference.md:15` — `222 MHz by firmware default, 333 MHz allowed`.
- **16 KB instruction + 16 KB data caches, 64-byte lines, 2-way, no L2.** [M] `cache_work.md:5` — `16 KB instruction and 16 KB data caches and no L2`; [M] `hw_capabilities.md:18` — `16 KB I and D caches, 64-byte lines, 2-way`; [M] `psp_hardware_reference.md:15` — `16 KB instruction cache, 16 KB data cache`.
- **Random cache-line miss ≈73 cycles at 333 MHz; sequential line ≈57.** [M] `psp_hardware_reference.md:17` — `Random cache-line miss ≈ 73 cycles at 333 MHz`; [M] `cpu_cost_map.md:48` — `~73 cycles for a random line, ~57 for a sequential one`. **Corroborated by another author's bench on a PSP-1000 [D, 2026-11-15]:** vfpu-docs' memory test (lab `external`: vfpu-docs f283bbb `docs/reference-outputs/mem_cycles.txt`, source `psp-tests/performance/main.c`) reads a dependent random chain at 72.5 cycles an access past 4 MB, and a sequential stream of word loads at 4.52 cycles a word past the cache, which is 55.7 cycles a line above the loads' own cycle. Its `LV.Q` stream's "2.0 bytes/cycle" is **not** a faster path: that loop reads 2,048 bytes an iteration while stepping 1,024 (`main.c`'s `LVQ_THR` at offsets 0, 512, 1,024, 1,536, each 512 bytes), so every line is read twice; the line's miss works out at 55.3 cycles, the same as under `lw`. A quad load doesn't fetch a line any faster than a word load.
- **A quiet-bus miss is 216 ns (72 cycles); beside the GE reading main RAM it is 424 ns.** [M] `plan_memory_stalls.md:19` — `216 ns (72 cycles) with the bus quiet`; [M] `levers_next_b138.md:21` — `A miss costs 424 ns instead of 216`.
- **Miss cost also depends on distance: 55 cycles within 128 bytes of the last miss, 71 cycles at 1 KB or more.** [M] `order_file.md:11` — `one 1 KB or more away 71`.
- **A miss costs no more after the bus has been idle, and less when register work comes before it** (2026-11-11, fwbench round 2, both PSPs). Over a hit: 196 ns (65 cycles) straight behind another miss, 171 ns with 30 ns of register work between them, and 157-183 ns (52-61 cycles) from 120 ns to 16 µs of idle bus, with no step and the two units within 4 ns of each other on every row; past that the runs cross interrupts and nothing consistent shows out to 1 ms. So the RAM's power-down counter (Firmware section) costs play's misses nothing, and the first ~25 ns (8 cycles) of register work after a miss is free: the next miss would have waited that long anyway (the fill's tail, most likely [U]). In this loop a load in address order was never cheaper than one 2 KB on, at any gap: it doesn't show the near price above, which belongs to a walk's next line straight away. [M, PSP-1000 and PSP-3000] `lab:psp/sierra/findings/measurements.tsv:12833` — `196 ns straight behind another miss; no step`; `lab:psp/sierra/findings/measurements.tsv:12834` — `Within 4 ns of the PSP-1000 on every row to 16 us`.
- **The core is in-order: it stalls at the missing load, only ~10 cycles of work slip under a miss, there is never more than one miss in flight, and there is no hardware prefetcher.** [M] `cpu_cost_map.md:49` — `The core is in-order`; `never has two misses in flight`.
- **Data prefetch `cache 30` (Fill, 0x1E) is real and non-blocking, but pays only with ≥~70 cycles of independent work; fills do not overlap each other; there is no MIPS `pref`.** [M] `cpu_cost_map.md:50` — `It pays only with ≥ ~70 cycles of independent work`; [M] `discussion_2026-10-14.md:60` — `hides 80–90% of a miss`. **Refined by halo_bench17** (PSP-1000, bench-hw 42869850188, 2026-11-10) [M]: "independent" means no load, store or instruction miss between the prefetch and the use (they all wait behind the fill); register work of any unit counts alike: VFPU (`vmul.q`), FPU (`mul.s`) and the integer loop around them hide the same share. One line's miss is ~70 cycles; ~56 cycles of register work after the prefetch hid 88-89% of it, ~110 cycles all of it, on both units. So the VFPU isn't what makes the shape pay, and an FPU-only core can use it (the ME's own cache: unmeasured).
- **No instruction prefetch is possible from user code: `cache 0x1E` fills no I-cache line, and the I-side op raises "coprocessor unusable" in user mode.** [M] `cpu_cost_map.md:51` — `fills no I-cache line`; [M] `hw_capabilities.md:21` — `none possible from user code`.
- **`cache 24` (create dirty exclusive, 0x18) makes a line without reading it, at half the cost of writing whole lines: 1 MB 6,067 → 3,116 µs; 172 → 355 MB/s.** [M] `cpu_cost_map.md:52` — `half the cost of writing whole lines`; [M] `research/research_sh4zam_crosscheck.md:42` — `172 → 355 MB/s`.
- **`cache 0x19` (D hit invalidate) runs in user mode and drops a dirty line without writing it back** (2026-11-11): a line written back as 0x11111111, then stored 0x22222222 and dropped, reads 0x11111111. On an 8 KB dirty scratch evicted by a 32 KB read, dropping it first saves ~83 cycles a line (211.8 → 179.8 µs an iteration, −15%); with `cache 24` before the writes as well, 154.8 µs (−27%). [M, PSP-1000] `lab:psp/sierra/findings/measurements.tsv:12793` — `CACHE 0x19 (HIT INVALIDATE) RUNS IN USER MODE AND DROPS A DIRTY LINE`.
- **Cache-op codes, from the firmware's own use: D index load tag 0x10 returns both ways' tags; 0x11/0x13 store tag; hit ops 0x18–0x1E; I hit invalidate 0x08; I index store tag 0x01/0x03; I fill 0x0E faults in user mode *(2026-11-11: the I fill Sony's 6.61 code uses is 0x0A, kernel-only, measured as a fill on the ME, Media Engine section; what 0x0E is stays [U])*; D hit invalidate 0x19 is allowed in user mode [M, above]; no source names a cache-lock op (0x0F/0x1F likeliest; Sony's 6.61 never uses an instruction-side lock, the firmware session's read).** [D]/[U] `redesign_tick_target.md:270` — `No source names a lock op`.

## Pipeline and scheduling

The Allegrex is a MIPS 4K-based core: in-order, single-issue, and every interlock is a slip (the pipeline waits; nothing is reordered). The FPU's latencies are measured (fpubench, 2026-10-27); the integer ones are the 4K's, a best guess until a latency bench runs.

| First instruction | Then | Wait | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Load | its consumer | 1 cycle | [D] MIPS MD00016 (4K manual) Table 2-1 |
| `mult`/`madd` 32×32 | `mfhi`/`mflo` | latency 2 | [D] Table 2-5 |
| `mul` 32×32 | its consumer | 3 cycles | [D] Table 2-5 |
| `mfhi`/`mflo` | its consumer | 1 cycle | [D] Table 2-5 |
| `div` | `mfhi`/`mflo` | 10–34 by operand size | [D] Table 2-5 |

| FPU divide / square root | its consumer | ~31 / ~33 cycles, not pipelined | [M, PSP-3000] `lab:psp/sierra/findings/measurements.tsv:9338` — `not pipelined; 5.2 when the dividend is zero` |
| `trunc.w.s` + `mfc1` | its consumer | 11.1 cycles | [M, PSP-3000] `lab:psp/sierra/findings/measurements.tsv:9340` — `trunc.w.s and mfc1 (float to integer)` |
| VFPU ops | — | measured: arithmetic 5, reductions 7, compares 8, matrix product N²+6, division 14N+3 | [D] pspdev vfpu-docs `inst-vfpu-desc.yaml`, measured by its authors on PSPs |

- **The compiler doesn't know any of this.** pspdev's GCC 15.2.0 treats `-march=allegrex` as MIPS II with no pipeline description and no `-mtune`, so it schedules with the generic fallback [D, GCC source]. Inline assembly (all the VFPU code) is never scheduled by GCC.
- **A profiler counter read alone gives the same counts as the block of 19, and the system call is most of a read's cost.** D-misses identical over the same work, cycles and memory stall within 0.25%; each counter read is itself an uncached load. One kernel call reading all 19 takes 1.49 µs, one call reading one word 0.51 µs. [M, PSP-1000] `lab:psp/sierra/findings/measurements.tsv:13748` — `single words read the same counts as the block`.
- **What it costs, measured:** the profiler's stall counters put internal stalls at 6.1–8.1% and coprocessor (FPU) stalls at 3.0–5.6% of the main CPU's cycles on seven intros and three routes [M] `hardware_utilization.md` §2. That is the ceiling a pipeline model attacks; some of it sits on true dependency chains, so the recoverable share is a fraction [U]. The lead and its path (`-mtune=4kc` as a proxy, a latency bench, then an Allegrex description) are in the lab's research notes on the compiler.

## FPU and VFPU

- **Single-precision FPU only; doubles are software (`__extendsfdf2`/`__muldf3`), with 1,097 extend sites counted.** [M]/[D] `psp_hardware_reference.md:15` — `doubles are software`.

## Firmware (6.61, read in the PSP-1000's own modules)

Read, not measured: what Sony's 6.61 kernel modules and the PSP-1000's Media Engine image do, from listings of Sheepdog01's own unit's flash0 (2026-11-11). Offsets are `module@offset` into the module's first segment; the lab's page has each one. These say how the system behaves; none is a price.

- **The VFPU's context (576 bytes) moves only on a switch to a thread that carries the VFPU attribute and isn't the unit's owner; the FPU's is lazy (saved on first use by another thread).** A thread without the attribute never disturbs the owner. [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/mining_2026-11-11.md:52` — `it saves the owner's 576 bytes and loads the new thread's`.
- **The kernel's timer fires together everything due within 200 µs of the earliest sleeper,** so a `sceKernelDelayThread` of 50 or 100 µs is a ~200 µs sleep (the lab's measured 205 µs). [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/mining_2026-11-11.md:91` — `fires together everything due within 200 µs of the earliest sleeper`.
- **me-core's `kcall` runs `sceKernelIcacheInvalidateAll` before every call:** that walk is the 17.1 µs of an empty kcall (Main CPU section) and it leaves the instruction cache empty for the code that follows. [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/mining_2026-11-11.md:26` — `begin with `sceKernelIcacheInvalidateAll()``.
- **One store raises the other CPU's interrupt, in both directions: `0xBC100044 = 1` between two `sync`s.** From the ME it is interrupt 31 on the main CPU; Sony's own job ends are signalled this way, with no polling. [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/mining_2026-11-11.md:89` — `Written by the main CPU it interrupts the ME; written by the ME it raises interrupt 31 on the main CPU`.
- **Sony's idle ME halts with no lost-wake window:** interrupts off, a look at the pending flag, `halt`, interrupts on; a wake that lands in between stays pending and ends the halt. [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/mining_2026-11-11.md:95` — `it turns interrupts off, looks at the pending flag, and executes `halt` with them still off`.
- **`sceGeListUpdateStallAddr` is about twenty instructions and, for the running list, one store to `0xBD40010C`;** the GE goes on by itself when that register moves. [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/mining_2026-11-11.md:73` — `one store to `0xBD40010C` and a `sync``.
- **`sceKernelCpuSuspendIntr` / `ResumeIntr` are not system calls for a user program:** an inline `mfic`/`mtic` pair in user mode. [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/mining_2026-11-11.md:209` — `Kernel_Library's versions run inline in user mode`.
- **The GE's finish interrupt copies a 2 KB context only for a list queued with one;** pspgu queues none. [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/mining_2026-11-11.md:210` — `copies a 2 KB context only for lists queued with one`.
- **A stick read is direct only when its buffer is 64-byte aligned and both its ends are on sectors:** an unaligned buffer bounces every sector, a 4-aligned one the first and last, and a part-sector end goes through a one-sector buffer; each piece is a command with the fixed cost (Memory Stick section). [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/mining_2026-11-11.md:108` — `not 4-byte aligned, every sector is bounced`.
- **Reads of 32 KB or more on a PRO stick open a sequential stream that the next read continues only if it starts at the exact next sector;** 30 ms idle, or any small read or write, closes it. [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/mining_2026-11-11.md:110` — `only if it starts at the exact next sector`.
- **Before every signal to the ME, Sony's main-CPU side flushes the DDR controller** (`sync`, a mask to `0xBD000004`, a spin until it clears), and its hand-shake words live in the uncached shared block at `0xBFC00600`, not in main RAM; the ME's firmware never touches the DDR controller. The port's `sync`-only hand-over has not been benched against it (Still open: the write buffer). [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/mining_2026-11-11.md:193` — `then a mask written to `0xBD000004` and a spin until it reads back clear`.
- **The sample-rate-converter audio path has no kernel mixer thread:** DMA reads the caller's buffer and the rate is a code in a hardware register; the kernel's mixer thread (priority 5, a wake every 64 frames) runs only for ordinary channels. [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/mining_2026-11-11.md:207` — `On that path the firmware has no software mixer and no mixer thread`.
- **The DDR controller's power-down counter (`0xBD00002C`) is on with count 4 in play, and nothing in 6.61 changes it at run time** (2026-11-11): the DDR driver has a setter and a getter for the word, and only the power module calls them, to save it before a suspend and put it back after. The count's unit isn't known, and ~~whether a miss that follows an idle bus pays for it isn't measured (Still open)~~ a miss that follows an idle bus doesn't pay for it (measured 2026-11-11 on both PSPs, Main CPU section). [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/hardware_potential.md:115` — `with a setter and a getter in Sony's driver`; [M] `psp_hardware_reference.md:253` — `2c=80000004`.
- **Sony's ME codec runs `cache 0x1C` on two lines of its stack frame on the way in and `cache 0x16` on them on the way out,** and the kernel keeps a third word of state a set beside the two tags (ops `0x12`/`0x13`): the shape of a line made without a fill and locked into its set, then released. What the two ops do exactly isn't measured (Still open). [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/hardware_potential.md:150` — `on two lines of its stack frame on the way in`.
- **A user program's bus clock is always half the PLL:** the power module ignores any other bus figure asked for. [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/mining_2026-11-11.md:213` — `the power module sets the bus to half the PLL whatever is asked`.
- **The ME image's code is 513,828 bytes from `0x88300000`, and me-core calls into it,** so the ME partition's first ~502 KB can't be reused while the library runs. **[2026-11-15, narrower: the port's shipped build makes no call into the image:** its one jump, the software-interrupt routine at `+0x13230`, is reached from `me_wake()` only under `mehalt` (off by default), and its one read is the 4-byte witness word at `+0x18` at each launch and restart (`psp_me.c`, `me_lib_reset`). By the port's own code the whole partition is unreferenced once the port has reset the ME; whether the firmware's side leaves it alone through a session, a wake and an exit is open (the lab's brief memory_meside, item B). [D] the lab's or-job 43682281701, lines checked.]** [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/mining_2026-11-11.md:200` — `the image's code is 513,828 bytes from `0x88300000``.
- **A system call's dispatch is about 100 instructions (404 bytes, 6.3 code lines); it saves no context and writes the two memory-protection registers on the way in and out.** The 0.54 µs of a call that does nothing (Main CPU section) is that path with its lines resident; in play a call also fetches them again and pushes the game's lines out, so the count of calls is the only part a program can avoid. [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/firmware_side_review.md:17` — `the dispatch is about 100 instructions`. **Made exact 2026-11-15 [D]:** one pair of writes, on the way out only: after the called routine returns, the dispatch stores the constant `0xCCCCCCCC` into `0xBC000000` and `0xBC000004` (kernel read and write on, user read and write off, for every 256 KB block of physical `0x08000000-0x083FFFFF`). So no setting of those two words outlives the next system call's return: **the kernel's 3 MB and the ME's 1 MB partition can't be opened to user mode and held open** on 6.61, by `sceKernelSetDdrMemoryProtection` or otherwise; kernel-mode code and the ME reach them always. `lab:docs/research/fw661/reference_read.md:121` — `stores the constant 0xCCCCCCCC into both words`.
- **There is no user-mode clock.** `sceKernelGetSystemTimeLow` is three instructions, one load of the timer register at `0xBC600000`, behind a system call; `sceKernelGetSystemTimeWide` is 36 with interrupts off. The register is in kernel space and the CPU's own counter is privileged (the load alone is 0.14 µs in kernel mode, Main CPU section). [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/firmware_side_review.md:28` — `there is no user-mode clock`.
- **`sceDmacMemcpy` puts its caller to sleep until the DMA's interrupt, and its calls are serialised:** one copy at a time, and the calling thread does nothing else meanwhile. [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/firmware_side_review.md:60` — `the caller sleeps until the DMA's interrupt`.
- **`sceDmacMemcpy` takes any alignment and sizes its transfers by what the two addresses share:** 16 bytes when source and destination agree in their low four bits, narrower when they don't; unaligned head and tail bytes are copied by the CPU, and it refuses only null pointers, a zero length and addresses a user may not touch (2026-11-11). So a source at a 16-aligned offset inside a 64-aligned buffer copies as fast as the buffer's start. [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/mining_2026-11-11.md:141` — `sizes its transfers by what the two addresses' low four bits share`.
- **The kernel's own block fill and copies never fetch a line they are about to overwrite:** for a 4-byte-aligned destination in a cached segment and a length over 256 bytes they run `cache 0x18` on each whole 64-byte line and then store its sixteen words; the head and tail go plainly (2026-11-11). The op runs in user mode too and saves ~65 cycles a line (Main CPU section). The port's `memcpy` and `memset` don't do this (the lab's lever CLAIMFILL). [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/hardware_potential.md:58` — `on every whole destination line before the sixteen stores that fill it`.
- **On a PSP-1000 the ME image that runs under a homebrew is `meimg`:** the wrapper module picks one of three images by a mode argument, and me-core's PSP-1000 offsets fit only `meimg` (the port's `mehalt` wakes through one of them and works on the PSP-1000 [M]). [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/me_image_psp1000.md:177` — `picks by a mode argument on a PSP-1000`.
- **Sony's ME image does nothing for its own instruction cache that a program could copy:** it sets no cache mode (CP0's Config is only read), runs its code from main RAM at `0x88300000` with eDRAM holding data only, writes no TLB register, never touches the DDR controller, and uses the instruction fill (`cache 0x0A`) only just before a halt and after a request's interrupt, to have the code it wakes into resident. [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/me_image_psp1000.md:187` — `The image never writes CP0's Config register`; `lab:docs/research/fw661/me_image_psp1000.md:188` — `Its code does not run from eDRAM`.
- **Nothing in the DDR controller's block (`0xBD000000`) decides who gets the bus** (2026-11-11): its words are the flush (`+0x04`), a set-up command sequencer, the power-down counter (`+0x2C`, above), timing fields loaded by PLL setting at a clock change, and a reset and calibration sequence. No word is indexed by a bus master and nothing in the driver or the power module reads the load, so the GE's and DMA's tolls on a miss (Bus and contention) are the bus being held, with no setting to change them. Tightening the timing fields would be overclocking by another name: not touched. [D] `lab:docs/research/fw661/hardware_potential.md:194` — `No word is indexed by a bus master`.

## Settled (were open in the records; the answer is in the section named)

- **Why 13 VME opcodes are dead on the PSP-1000** (settled 2026-11-11 as far as it can be without a source: not power, not Sony's set-up; most likely absent units [U]): VME section.
- **Bus arbiter:** no knob exists; closed by a register snapshot and the DDR driver's disassembly (Bus and contention, 2026-11-04/07).

- **A direct GE-bus counter:** none; 0x50 reads 0 under load (Bus and contention, 2026-11-07).
- **The VME on the PSP-1000:** computes exactly, with no firmware call (VME, round 5, 2026-11-01).
- **The ME after a sleep:** restarts after the game's own stops on the PSP-1000; real sleeps pass (Media Engine, 2026-10-29).

- **The ME's I-cache geometry:** 16 KB, 2-way, 64-byte lines, LRU (Media Engine).
- **GE lists from VRAM:** sub-lists execute from VRAM, the cheapest placement (GE).
- **Whether the two CPUs slow each other:** by load: latency-bound ME work doesn't, scattered ME misses cost the tick 10–11% (Bus and contention).
- **The FPU's latencies:** measured (Pipeline and scheduling, 2026-10-27).
