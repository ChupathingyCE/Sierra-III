## 1. The target, and where the tick goes

**Target:** a bare tick of ≤ 25.8 ms on b30's flight, with drawing, other and frame end at ~15 ms a frame; ≤ 22.3 if they end at ~20 (plan §1). Today it's **42.4 ms bare** (b151's ledger1 bench, PSP-3000, 2026-11-05; ledger 3 less the instruments' ~7% had estimated 40.6).

**Where it goes** (b30's flight, PSP-3000 sampler, 42114568213) [M]: collision 25.6%, AI 16.5, node matrices and animation 11.3, units and vehicles 9.3, other objects 7.7, libm and memcpy 5.3, effects 3.4, tags 2.9, audio 2.4, scripts 1.6, the rest 14.5. It waits on memory for 56-68% of its cycles on every stretch, its instruction and data misses close to even in cost (`cpu_cost_map.md` §2).

**What changes tick to tick** (C3) [E for counts, M for times]:
- objects: 69-73% unchanged on b30, 96-97% on b40;
- object updates' time (PSP-1000, 42141971439): 34.9 ms a tick, 6.8 of it (19%) in updates that left the object unchanged; the moving bipeds 22.9 (~970 µs each); **Corrected 2026-11-16 (audit, after Qwen4 Max's read):** these ms come from a census build whose tick was ~47-60 ms, not the shipped 39.9 ms; as shares of that tick (object updates 58-74%), bipeds are ~16-21 ms of the shipped tick [U], so a pot sized as a share of 24.3 ms runs 15-70% high.
- collision's queries mostly don't repeat (vectors 3-6%, reconnects under 5%); bipeds' feature-in-sphere queries do (25-46% for bipeds; 16-39% overall);
- **b40's snowfield, timed** (PSP-3000, sierra-hw 42152471353, game time 14,100-15,300; row c3_objcost_ms_b40_snowfield) [M]: object updates 30.8 ms a tick, 6.6 (21%) leaving the object unchanged: **vehicles 3.7** (6.4 a tick at ~580 µs: gun turrets and parked vehicles), machines 0.9, weapons 0.7, scenery 0.4, bodies 0.6. Bipeds 20.0 ms (24.9 a tick); **far and out of the fight 28% of it, ~5.5 ms a tick**; in the fight 57%;
- bipeds by distance and fight (objcost2 42146294460, shares of biped time): far and out of the fight 19% on b30's flight (~4.6 ms a tick at the PSP's 24.3), 32% on b40's snowfield, 14% on the cliff, 8% on b30's landing; in the fight at 20-40 units 30-38% everywhere; bodies without an AI 25% on the landing, 86-97% unchanged.

**Re-aimed by the PSP's own split (2026-11-09, Sheepdog01 08:39: only the PSP says where time goes).** On b30's beach fight replayed by the pad recorder, the PSP-3000 put the object, unit and biped updates at 49% of the tick and the main CPU's AI at 13%, where the emulator had said 32% and 37% (lab findings `combat_steps_psp_vs_emu`) [M]. Its largest steps: node matrices 6.1 ms a tick, biped physics 4.1, `unit_verify_vectors` 3.2, the per-type updates 2.8, the object loop 2.0. So the per-object update is S's first ground, and the ceilings above built on emulator shares (objcost2's biped bands) are re-measured on the PSP before a form is chosen: S4's band census runs there next. S4's half-rate form also needs another shape: the engine's per-tick constants (`TICKS_PER_SECOND` in `bipeds.c`: velocities, landing speeds, turn rates) leave no time step to double [D].

### S1. The unchanged-object skip

An object whose update leaves it bitwise unchanged doesn't need the update. The work is knowing that *before* the update runs, with a predicate that's never wrong:

- **Already done this way:** the sleepers (FIXES_WS: ~217 of b30's ~274 scenery a tick never enter `psp_object_update`; recon, 18:57) and the resting weapons and equipment (IS). S1 extends the same idea to the kinds that still carry the measured 6.8 ms:
  - **not vehicles, after all** (recon's predicate read, whyawake1 42152687593 [E]): the 2.4 ms of "unchanged" vehicle updates on b30's flight are the Pelicans hovering on a script, not at rest (~1.1 a tick); parked Warthogs already sleep or cost ~11 µs. A scripted vehicle's input is its script, so it stays out of S1;
  - **awake scenery** (2.3 ms, 53 a tick at ~42 µs) **isn't unchanged: it's animated** (recon's read, whyawake2 42153640742 [E]): its node matrices change every tick (b30's leafy trees, c10's plants and lily pads); the play hash leaves node matrices out, which is why objcost counted it unchanged. Skipping it isn't exact. What's left for S1 is its update's non-pose work (emitters' timers, a few function words), not yet split; recon times it next. **An exact angle on the pose itself** [U]: at the floor's two ticks a frame only the drawn tick's pose is seen, so a tree's node matrices could be made only on the drawn tick and on any read (LBM's machinery). LBM lost on bipeds because the AI reads their poses every tick (~1.2 ms a tick of reads, `levers.tsv`); trees may be read rarely. **Counted** (whyawake3 42155122122 [E]; row s_slice2_scenery_pose): node matrices are 36% of awake scenery's update on b30 and 48% on c10 (c10's plants and lily pads, ~3.0 ms a tick of scenery updates in the emulator). On **c10 nothing reads them on the undrawn tick** (0 reads in 150 undrawn ticks), so poses made only on the drawn tick and on a read are exact there and halve that pose time (~0.7 ms a tick [U]). On **b30 bipeds and code outside the object loop read them** (~6,900 reads in 150 undrawn ticks), so LBM's lesson holds and b30 gains nothing. A target-side piece for c10, not a floor piece for the heavy stretches;
  - **bodies** (25% of the biped time on the landing, 86-97% unchanged): the corpse sleep (b105-b114) already offers every settled body; what keeps some awake is its own guard, a body held up by something other than the level (b30 7.7 of ~50 a tick, d20 12.3 of 36; 55-440 µs each in the emulator). **Slice 1 is therefore:** a body held up by objects may sleep while everything holding it is asleep or static, and wakes the tick any of them wakes or goes (recon, building under `skipchange`).
- **The wake rules** are the sleepers': contact, damage, a script, a parent that moved, a force. A wrong predicate shows up as a play difference, so each kind's rule is proven by play and both seeds identical on b30, c10, c20 and d20 (statecrc), with a check build that runs the update anyway and compares (as `rsocheck` and `vislazycheck` do).

### S2. Collision's feature-query reuse: the level half

The repeating queries are bipeds' `collision_get_features_in_sphere` calls. **Census** (recon, s2census1 42155463319 [E]; row s_slice3_census):
- **the level's part is most of a call:** its BSP sphere test and features are 71% of the call's time on b30 (64 + 2 of 93 µs), 61% on b40, 76% on c20, 50% on d20; the objects' part is the rest;
- **the level never changes**, so its half of the answer is exact to reuse whenever the sphere and the query's flags are bitwise the same and no breakable surface has changed (glass that breaks changes the level's answer). That holds on **31-47% of biped feature queries**, more than the 19-39% that also need every object unchanged;
- **the objects' half can't be keyed cheaply:** collidable objects change their node matrices without a reconnect (held weapons, trees, b40's turret, some grunts), so a per-cluster stamp would be inexact. It stays live (recomputed every query) in S2's form;
- **memory is small:** a biped's answer is 2-3 features, ~250-300 bytes on average (at most 4.3 KB on b30): a level-half store of ~1 KB a caller covers nearly all, ~30 KB for b30's bipeds (the heap has ~69 KB free there; a scratchpad candidate only in part).

**Worth** [U]: the sphere test and feature gathering are ~8.5% of b30's flight tick (~3.4 ms of 40.6), the level part ~70% of that, reused on ~31-47%: **~0.75-1.1 ms a tick.**

**Proof:** play and both seeds identical; a check build compares every reused level answer with a fresh walk (as slice 1).

- **Its copies are memcpy's misses** [E counts, the lab's dcanat2 43011966093, research/opportunities/footprint.md §5]: `psp_aime_think_actor` 1,252 misses a tick on b30's flight (134 copies of ~386 B) and `psp_pose_ahead_take_work` 604 (43 of ~434 B), 90% of memcpy's misses there (92% on c20); ~0.4-0.8 ms a tick at the PSP's miss price [U]. The fix on trial keeps such an end and makes it on the main CPU at the next tick (`aitickoldconvdefer`, bottleneck/s3fix 7bc6472c; the lab's s3o27_b40). The redesign's hand-over should list every pool the think can write (conversations, props, the recent-conversation table, the firing-position sort pointer of B69) and give each a record or a main-CPU step.

### S4. The far-biped tier (ruling 3)

For bipeds far from the player and out of the fight (AIFAR's test), two forms, chosen by a count and a side-by-side:
- **half rate:** updated every other tick with twice the time step, as the AI's think already is at half rate (aicad3). Saves half their time (~2.3 ms a tick on b30's flight, more on the snowfield). Risk: physics with a doubled step (a fall, a slope); proven by side-by-sides and the outcome census at the stretches where far bipeds move.
- **on the ME:** their whole update moved, so the main CPU saves all of it (~4.6 ms a tick on b30's flight, less what the tier test leaves out). It needs ME room after S3; its shape is below, so it comes second.

**The ME form's shape** (2026-11-06, for recon's 01:53 question) [U until the census]. Not a world snapshot: **the tier is defined so that a member reads nothing that moves this tick except itself**, and then its update needs no old world at all.
- **Who's in:** far and out of the fight (AIFAR's test) *and*, at the tick's start, no awake object other than static scenery within its reach (its physics sphere plus the tick's largest step and a margin), not airborne, and no pending damage or script on it. The tier test runs on the main CPU at the tick's start; a biped that fails it this tick updates on the main CPU as today. That's the tier's definition, not a fallback: who belongs is decided before the work starts, never after a failure.
- **What it reads:** its own datums (object header, object, unit, biped, node matrices, animation state), copied to the ME; the level's BSP and collision geometry, static and read where they are; tags, read-only; static scenery's collision within reach (the census sizes it). So **collision is the same code against the same world**: the level and static objects don't change during the tick, and nothing that moves is within reach.
- **Where it lives:** the copies in eDRAM (`redesign_b.md` §3.2), a few KB a biped, double-buffered; the ME runs the tier's updates while the main CPU walks everyone else in the same tick.
- **What it writes:** its own datums, copied back and installed by the main CPU at its slot's turn in the walk, so later objects see it moved as today; anything that allocates or touches shared state (effects, sounds, footsteps, the global seed, cluster reconnects) is queued by the ME and done by the main CPU at that slot, in walk order. A member that would draw the global seed is the census's question: if far, grounded bipeds draw it, the draws are replayed in order or the tier excludes that state.
- **Timing:** the ME must finish a member before the main CPU's walk reaches its slot; members go first in the ME's queue and the wait is bounded and logged (a miss is fixed where it runs).
- **Exactness:** it's the same update on the same inputs, with only the cost moved; the side effects land in walk order. If the census bears that out, S4's ME form is exact, not "plays the same", and it needs no tick behind.
- **The census it needs** (recon, strict, b30's flight and the snowfield), for far out-of-the-fight bipeds: the share that pass the tier test (awake non-static objects within reach, airborne, damage); what their update reads outside their own datums, by kind and bytes; their side effects per update (effects, sounds, seed draws, reconnects); and the bytes of their datums. It sizes the eDRAM's share and the tier's take.
- **The census, counted (recon, 2026-11-06/07; carried in 2026-11-15 from the branch wargame/hwfacts, where these lines had stayed when the page forked; the counts are exact [E], the ms figures predate the PSP's re-aim above and are leads):**
  - **far1's first counts** (recon, sierra-batch 42186210647 [E], fast interpreter): of far out-of-the-fight updates, **17% pass on b30's flight** (3,193 of 18,305) and **53% on b40 to the snowfield** (7,144 of 13,405). b30's fail mostly on objects in reach (10,569 objects only, 1,238 vertical motion only, 3,305 both); b40's are mixed (2,614 / 2,426 / 1,221). A passing update draws the global seed 1 time in 25-50 (0.020 b30, 0.042 b40), allocates no effects, starts an impulse sound 0.014 times (b40), reconnects 2.5-2.9 times, makes ~1.0 feature query and reaches 1.1-1.6 objects; its datums are ~4.4-4.6 KB.
    - **What it's worth as tested** [U on E × M]: b30's flight ~0.8 ms a tick (17% of the far bipeds' ~4.6), less than the half-rate form's ~2.3 over all of them; b40's snowfield ~2.9 (53% of ~5.5). The two forms combine: members on the ME, the far bipeds that fail the test at half rate: b30 ~2.7, the snowfield ~4.2.
    - **Two refinements before the read trace:** (1) **a closed group:** far bipeds walk in squads, so most "objects in reach" are likely other far out-of-the-fight bipeds; if everything in reach is static or another member, the ME updates the group in walk order, exactly; (2) **airborne, not vertical motion:** a grounded biped on a slope moves vertically; only leaving the ground matters. Counts for both, and which call sites draw the seed (if it's the airborne or landing branch, the test already excludes it), decide the tier's final share.
  - **far2: the refined tier takes nearly all of them** (recon, sierra-batch 42193554142 [E]): of the updates failing on objects in reach, 99.7% on b30's flight (13,839 of 13,874) and 56% on b40 (2,135 of 3,835) have only other far out-of-the-fight bipeds or their children in reach, a closed group; the vertical-motion fails are slopes (b30 0 airborne of 1,238, b40 25 of 2,426). So the tier as refined (a closed group, not airborne) holds up to **~99.8% of far updates on b30 and ~87% on b40** (upper bounds: the splits overlap). Its worth: b30's flight up to ~4.6 ms a tick, the snowfield ~4.8 [U on E × M].
    - **A group runs whole, in walk order, on the ME:** its members' results are installed at each member's slot.
    - **The global seed is the remaining wall:** the draws come from `animation_choose_random_permutation_internal` (b30 60 of 60, b40 295 of 301; the rest real_random_range, actor_died, random_vector_in_cone3d, item_accelerate), an animation's permutation picked when it starts, about once in 25-50 updates. The ME can't know the seed's state at a member's slot before the main CPU's walk gets there. Two ways:
      - **exact:** a member that reaches a draw stops, and it and the rest of its group are updated by the main CPU at their slots. That's decided by the work itself, not a failure; its cost is the share of groups with a draw (with groups of ~5, ~10-20% of the tier's work) [U], counted by the build;
      - **plays the same:** the animation's pick from a stream of the object's own, so the global seed's order changes. A ruling-3 choice, Sheepdog01's, if the exact form's share costs too much.
    - **far3, the read trace** (recon, sierra-batch 42197156088 [E]; object_get and object_get_and_verify_type, not the header's inline lookups): the tier's updates read themselves and their own weapon almost wholly; group members 11.0k (b30) and 8.0k (b40) reads, mostly collision; still scenery 19.2k and 27.2k, all collision; **moving objects outside the group: 0 on b30, 20 on b40** (scattered single callers). Groups are small: b30 ~9 members a tick in groups of 1-2 (522 of 3-4, none larger); b40 ~6, mostly alone. **The exact form's cost:** groups that draw the global seed in a tick are 2.9% on b30 (3.3% of members) and 4.5% on b40 (5.2%). So the exact form keeps ~95-97% of the tier on the ME, and the per-object stream (Sheepdog01's option) isn't needed.
    - **Where it runs is a placement** (plan §1a): the ME form moves ~4.5 ms a tick onto the ME, which pays where the ME has room after R (the snowfield, the beach, d20's walk) and not on b30's flight (the ME ~1,064 after R). There S4 takes its half-rate form, which removes work instead of moving it.

Collision stays the same code against the same world (a choice that keeps statecrc as S3's proof, not a multiplayer requirement: since Sheepdog01's 2026-11-09 17:30 ruling collision's answers need only keep hit reports and movement inside the host's bounds, CLAUDE.md). The tier is local: in the campaign every biped is the PSP's own; in co-op the host runs the far AI.

**What S4 doesn't reach:** bipeds in the fight at 20-40 units are the largest band (30-38% of biped time). A distance tier leaves them alone, since they're what the player is fighting. If S1-S4 land below their ceilings, they're where the margin sits; the plan's ruling 3 says what comes next (§5).

**S4's band census on the PSP (2026-11-09, PSP-1000, the b30 pad bench, master/qrtock b6d285a: lab sierra-hw 42672087890, findings `s4_far_out_share_*`) [M, shares; the census inflates the tick]:** far (20+) bipeds not in the fight are **30.3%** of the bipeds' update time on the flight, **12.8%** on the landing and **5.6%** on the beach fight, where all of it is actor-less bodies at ~0.14 ms each. In the fight, at every distance, is 84% of the beach's biped time (under 10: 31%, 10-20: 27%, 20-40: 26%). So the tier as shaped is a flight-and-landing lever, worth ~1 ms of the combat tick at most; combat's biped cost is the update itself, for bipeds the player is fighting. That points S at making each biped's update cheaper (`biped_update_physics`, `object_compute_node_matrices`, `unit_verify_vectors`: the PSP's top steps, combatsteps) rather than at updating fewer, and keeps any rate cut for in-fight bipeds a compromise behind Sheepdog01's A/B.

## 4. Proofs and boundaries

- Exact pieces (S1, S2, S5): play and both seeds identical, statecrc, on b30, c10, c20 and d20; check builds that run the old path beside the new and compare.
- "Plays the same" pieces (S3, S4): side-by-sides and the outcome census (the rule of 2026-10-30), with statecrc's identity no longer the proof; both sides of their A/B carry the new maths.
- The multiplayer boundary (corrected 2026-11-09, Sheepdog01 17:30; CLAUDE.md): it is the netcode's, not bit-exactness. Nothing a client computes is compared bit for bit; collision, node matrices, AI and physics reach the host only through movement (3.5 units) and hit reports (3-6 units), both bounds-checked; what must match is discrete (datum indices at map load, the join's version and checksums). So no piece of S needs to be exact for multiplayer: exact pieces are proven by statecrc because it's the cheaper proof, the rest by side-by-sides and the outcome census. (The list this replaces, "the rules, the 30 Hz tick, collision's answers, tag indices, object slots", was stricter than the netcode.)

## 5. Risks and open questions

**What the milestone says** (2026-11-06 00:40, bottleneck's reading of the code at recon/skipchange 50863234) [D for the code, U for the costs]: S2 reused the level half in ~30% of biped queries on the flight, and the tick didn't move. The code shows where its cost may hide, none of which the emulator's estimate (walk time saved, on the emulator's clock) counted:
- **every non-reused query pays a keep** (~70% of them): the level half packed and copied into the biped's slot (up to 896 bytes, the leaves, the breakable flags), with the prisms' unused points zeroed. A moving biped pays it every query and never reuses it;
- **the slot table is ~27 KB** (24 slots of ~1.1 KB), over the 16 KB data cache, and **its slot search walks the 24 `caller` words, each on its own line**, up to three times a query (reuse, then keep's two lookups); with more than 24 bipeds asking, slots are given up in turn and thrash;
- **the walk it saves may cost less on the PSP than the emulator's 15 µs:** SCRATCHBSP holds the BSP's top 304 nodes in the scratchpad there.
**The run's own counts confirm it** (recon, 00:38 [M counts]): of 18,000 biped feature queries on the flight, the level half was reused in 5,950 (33%) and **kept in 11,829 (66%)**, so two queries in three pay the keep and never reuse it; 221 were too big. S1 had **about one body a tick** asleep on objects on the flight: its ~1-2 ms was a bound by subtraction, not a count, and the flight has nothing for it (a d20 piece).

What follows from the rule: the milestone prices the part once; it doesn't send S1 and S2 back as levers to tune. What it changes is S2's design, and the sum: without S1 and S2, S on b30's flight is ~10.9-15.7 of the -16.6 needed, so S3 and S4 (and S4's ME form) carry it, and the margin grows. The design S2 would need:
- **keep only on a repeat:** a 24-byte key per biped compared each query; the answer kept only when the key matched the last query (a biped standing still), so a moving biped pays a compare and nothing else;
- **the table split:** the callers' keys in one small array (one or two lines), the answers apart, sized to the bipeds that stand still (the census gives the count);
- **counts before time:** reused, kept and given-up per query from the run's own log, and the emulator's instruction counts of keep against walk, before any second pair.
