# Review: mepointsclaim, a points job the ME hasn't begun is not a late job (B56)

**What failed.** The frame's point physics (`mepoints`, `psp_fixups.py` FIXES_MEJOBS) is a race from both ends: the ME makes the list from its back, the main CPU's particle and contrail loops make the rest from its front, and the main CPU's word tells the ME where to stop. The job is pushed in `particles_update`, just after the AI job (aime2a) has gone to the ME for the record, and collected at `contrails_update`'s end. When the ME is inside one long actor's thinking (the AI job serves the ring only between actors), it hasn't begun the job by then. The loops have already made every point, so nothing is missing, but `psp_points_end` still waited for the ticket: the wait ran out at the ring's 10 ms deadline, the job was reported failed ("with none made, not begun or on its first, point 127 of 128": 127 is just the list's last index, where the ME would have started), and the ring rested 1-4 s as if the ME were stuck, sending every ring job of that second to the main CPU. In the PSP-1000 d20 run (3 of 3 failures) each was preceded by `ring job N (type 12) not done 10 ms after its push`.

**The fix** (keep word `mepointsclaim`, on by default; off word `mepointsclaimoff`):

- `psp_points_me_make`, on the ME, stores "begun, none made" (`generation << 16 | count`) in its word before its first look at the main CPU's word. An index of `count` passes no point to the loops, so the loops read it as before.
- `psp_points_end` stores its stop in the main CPU's word (as before), then reads the ME's word. If the ME's word doesn't carry this frame's generation, the job hasn't begun: when the ME reaches it, its first look finds the stop and it makes nothing, writing no answer. So the main CPU doesn't wait for that ticket, and nothing is reported or rested. If the word does carry the generation, the job has begun, and the wait is as before (at most one point, ~60-77 us on the ME).
- Why it's safe: each CPU stores its own word and then loads the other's, both through the uncached view with a `sync` between, so at least one of them sees the other's store (the store-then-load pattern). Either the main CPU sees the job begun and waits, or the ME sees the stop and makes nothing. A job left this way is popped later, finds the stop (or a newer generation), and touches neither the list nor the answers, so the next frame can write the list again. The ring needs no wait for a ticket: a push checks only room and busy slots.
- The 30 s `mepoints:` line counts these jobs ("N not begun by the loop's end"). The line is timed by a frame count (every 900 frames): it read `psp_microseconds`, which answers 0 under `notimers` (a default), so it never printed in a default build.

**What it doesn't do.** The ME still doesn't take its share of that frame's points: the loops make them, as the design always did for points the ME doesn't reach. Getting the ME to the job sooner (the ring served inside a long actor, AIMERINGLOOK) is separate: v2 is built and off, and its yields ran ~0 ring jobs.

**Proof.** Emulator [E]: the ME's code runs inside the push there, so the job is always begun and the new branch can't be taken. Play and both seeds match the base at every tick on b30's route (4,500 ticks) and d20 (2,700), which proves the code around it changes nothing. PSP-1000 [M], d20 to exitat120 (game time 3,488): 0 ring jobs late and no ring rest, against 3 failed points jobs, each followed by a rest, in the run before the fix. No ME stop before the exit. One run against one: the lates are rare, but every one of the 3 had this cause.
